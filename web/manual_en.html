6. Device Topology

info

This is where you draw where your LED strips physically are.

The topology is the only way the application knows where each LED is. It is used by spatial effects (Wave, Fire, Bloom…), by the center of radial effects, by calibration and by the Ambilight.

6.1 What Is the Topology

For OpenRGB, a strip is just a list of numbered LEDs. The topology adds their position in space: where each zone starts, which way it runs, and how long it is.

lightbulb

Positions matter, names don't

Zone names ("Desktop", "MonitorTop", "Back"…) are just labels and decide nothing. A wave travels from left to right because the LEDs are drawn from left to right; the Ambilight colors the top side of the screen because those LEDs are drawn on the top side of the screen rectangle. If the drawing matches your desk, everything else follows.

6.1.1 Which devices appear

  • Strips and linear devices (LINEAR) for which you turned on Topology Editor in the Devices page (chapter 8).
  • The zones and their LED counts come from OpenRGB: you don't create them by hand. If you change something in OpenRGB, use Reload from OpenRGB.
  • Keyboards don't appear here: they have their own editor (chapter 5, Keyboard Designer).

6.2 The Page

6.3 The Canvas

6.3.1 Getting around

  • Pan the view: drag with the middle mouse button (press the wheel).
  • Zoom: mouse wheel.
  • Automatic framing: when the page opens, the whole scene is framed, and it stays framed when you resize the window until you pan or zoom by hand. Reset View frames it again at any time.

6.3.2 Coordinates and units

  • X axis points right, Y axis points up, like on graph paper.
  • Units: if you don't enter a physical length, each LED takes 1 unit. If you enter the length in centimeters (see 6.5), 1 unit = 1 cm: it's best to do this for all zones, so the distances between strips are the real ones.
  • What matters is the relative position of the zones to each other, not where the origin is.

6.3.3 How LEDs are drawn

  • Each LED is a cell as long as its spacing; the cells of a zone sit side by side as on the real strip. The center of the cell is the point used by effects, calibration and the Ambilight.
  • The first and last cells show the LED index (0 and N-1).
  • The yellow arrow shows the direction the LEDs run in (from LED 0 to the last one).
  • A zone with a dashed red border overlaps another one: the warning "Zone overlaps detected!" also appears.

6.4 Placing Zones

Action How
Move Drag the zone with the left button (with Snap to Grid on, the position follows the grid size), or type Position X/Y in the inspector.
Fine movement Arrow keys move the selected zone by 1 unit, Shift + Arrow keys by 0.1.
Rotate R rotates by 90° (Shift + R the other way), or use the four Rotation buttons: → 0°, ↑ 90°, ← 180°, ↓ 270°.

tips_and_updates

Rotation or direction? Rotation orients the zone in space; direction tells which end LED 0 is wired to. If the zone is in the right place but the colors run backwards, change the direction, not the rotation. Calibration (6.8) shows this right away on the real LEDs.

6.5 Zone Inspector

Select a zone (click it on the canvas or in the Zones list) to see its properties.

Property Description
Zone Name Free label, just to recognize it.
Position X, Y The point where the zone starts, on its edge: with 0° rotation it's the top-left corner.
Rotation 0°, 90°, 180° or 270°.
Direction Forward (0→N) or Reverse (N→0).
Physical Length (cm) Optional. The real length of the strip: the spacing becomes length ÷ LED count and the zone is drawn in centimeters. Leave it empty to go back to 1 unit per LED.
Device, Zone Index, LED Count Read-only: they come from OpenRGB.

6.6 Effect Center

The effect center is the point radial effects (Bloom, Explosion, Plasma) start from. There is one for the whole scene, shown on the canvas by a cyan crosshair.

  • Drag the crosshair on the canvas, or type Center X / Center Y as percentages: 0% = left/bottom, 100% = right/top, relative to the box that contains all the zones (BBox).
  • Use the Quick Presets: Center (50, 50), Top (50, 90), Bottom (50, 10), Left (10, 50), Right (90, 50).
  • It stays where you put it: if you then move the zones, the crosshair doesn't move with them. Only the percentages change, recalculated on the new box. It moves only when you move it.
info

The gray rectangle (VBBox) is the "virtual" box used by effects: it is extended evenly around the center, so the effect reaches every LED even when the center is near an edge. It's normal for it to go beyond the zones; the info bar shows its size.

6.7 Screen (for the Ambilight)

The Screen panel tells the Ambilight where the monitor is in the scene. On the canvas it is the dashed cyan rectangle labeled SCREEN.

  • Automatic (labeled "SCREEN (automatic)"): until you place it yourself, the rectangle surrounds the LEDs of the devices enabled for the Ambilight, with your screen's proportions. It follows the zones when you move them.
  • By hand: drag a corner to resize it or the border to move it. From then on it stays where you put it. Fit places it on your strips as a starting point; Automatic forgets the one placed by hand.
  • The proportions always stay those of your real screen (the panel shows them).

How the Ambilight uses each LED, depending on where it is drawn:

LED Look in the editor What it reads
Near a side of the rectangle normal The edge of the screen on that side, in the stretch facing the LED.
Inside the rectangle with a white dot The point of the screen under it.
Outside the rectangle faded Nothing: the Ambilight leaves it alone.
tips_and_updates

Tip: press Fit, adjust the rectangle so the strips around the monitor sit on its sides, and save. A strip far from the monitor (for example under the desk) stays outside and is not used by the Ambilight.

6.8 Calibration

Calibration lets you check that the drawing matches reality. Press Calibration: concentric rings appear on the canvas around the effect center and the real LEDs light up exactly like the drawn ones.

  • The ring colors repeat: white, red, blue, green, then white again. The distance between one ring and the next is the value of the u field next to the button.
  • The LED closest to the center is circled in white on the canvas and blinks on the real strip: it's the quickest way to check where the center is.
  • In the Calibration Settings panel you choose which elements blink (center LED, rings of each color) and how fast.
  • Calibration follows every change in real time, even unsaved ones: move a zone, change direction or center, and the LEDs update.
  • While it is on, the Ambilight is paused. It turns off when you press the button again or leave the page.
fact_check

How to read it: look at a zone on the canvas and at the same strip on your desk. If the colors are the same but in reverse order, change the direction; if they are shifted, the zone is drawn in the wrong place. If the blinking LED isn't the circled one, that LED's zone is drawn wrong.

6.9 Saving

Save Topology saves the zones, the effect center and the screen rectangle to config/device_topologies.json. Changes apply right away to effects and the Ambilight: no restart needed.

save

Remember to save. Changes live only in the page until you save: if you close it, they are lost. Reset All discards them on purpose, reloading the last saved topology.

6.10 Example: Monitor with Strips on the Sides

screenshot_monitor

Hardware: one strip running around the monitor (left, top, right) and another one under the desk.

  1. In Devices, turn on Topology Editor for the strip, then open this page.
  2. For each zone, enter the physical length in cm: the zones take on their real size.
  3. Drag the zones to where they are on your desk and rotate them (R): the vertical sides at 90° or 270°, the top one at 0° or 180°.
  4. Turn on Calibration and check the real LEDs: if a zone runs backwards, press F.
  5. In the Screen panel, press Fit and adjust the rectangle on the three monitor strips; the strip under the desk stays outside.
  6. Place the effect center where you want radial effects to start.
  7. Save Topology.

6.11 Troubleshooting

6.11.1 The device doesn't appear

  • In the Devices page, Topology Editor must be turned on for that device.
  • It must be a linear device (keyboards have their own editor).
  • If the zones changed in OpenRGB, press Reload from OpenRGB.

6.11.2 Effects go the wrong way

  • Turn on Calibration and compare the canvas with the real LEDs.
  • Colors in reverse order: change the direction (F).
  • Zone oriented wrong: change the rotation (R).

6.11.3 Radial effects don't start from the right point

  • Use calibration to check which LED blinks: it's the one closest to the center.
  • Did you press Save Topology after moving the center?
  • The center only applies to radial effects (Bloom, Explosion, Plasma).

6.11.4 Calibration doesn't light up the LEDs

  • Check that OpenRGB is connected (indicator on the Home page).
  • If an error notification "Calibration: …" appears, the message tells you why.

6.11.5 The Ambilight colors the wrong side

  • The side is decided by the position of the LEDs relative to the screen rectangle (6.7), not by the zone name. Adjust the rectangle and save.

1. Introduction

rocket_launch

Welcome to ED:Event2Led!

This manual guides you step-by-step through installation, configuration and advanced use of the application for synchronizing your PC's RGB lighting with Elite Dangerous events.

1.1 What is ED:Event2Led

ED:Event2Led is a desktop application that monitors Elite Dangerous journal files in real time and translates in-game events into customized RGB lighting sequences for your PC devices (keyboard, mouse, LED strips, case fans, etc.).

The application connects to OpenRGB to control the hardware and provides a modern web interface to configure every aspect of the system.

1.1.1 Key Features

  • Real-Time Journal Monitoring — Automatically detects new journal files when you launch Elite Dangerous and reads events in real time while you play
  • OpenRGB Integration — Universal multi-brand RGB control (supports 100+ devices)
  • Multi-Step Effect Sequencer — Create complex animations by combining 10+ steps per effect
  • 17 Spatial Effects — From static transitions to fire simulation, matrix rain, plasma, aurora and lightning
  • Flexible Event Mapping — Assign custom effects to 100+ in-game events
  • System Status Integration — React to real-time ship flags (shields, heat, interdiction, hardpoints deployed)
  • 2D Keyboard Designer — Visualize the LED matrix, color individual keys, save presets
  • Device Topology — Draw where your LED strips are: effects follow the real layout
  • Ambilight — The strips around the monitor take the screen colors when no effect is running
  • Multilingual — IT, EN, DE, ES, FR

1.2 System Requirements

1.2.1 Hardware

  • Windows 10/11 PC (64-bit)
  • OpenRGB-compatible RGB devices (keyboards, mice, LED strips, case fans, RGB motherboards, etc.)
  • 4 GB RAM minimum (8 GB recommended for complex multi-device effects)

1.2.2 Software

  • OpenRGB version 0.8 or higher (download)
  • Elite Dangerous installed (required only for journal monitoring)
  • Python 3.11+ (if running from source)

1.3 Quick Installation

Step 1: Install OpenRGB

  1. Download OpenRGB from openrgb.org
  2. Extract the ZIP and launch OpenRGB.exe
  3. In the OpenRGB interface, go to Settings → SDK Server
  4. Enable "Start Server" and set port 6742 (default)
  5. Optional: Enable "Start minimized to tray" for automatic startup

Step 2: Launch ED:Event2Led

  1. Extract the ED:Event2Led package to a folder (e.g. C:\EDLogReader\)
  2. Launch start_dev_server.bat (or python src/main.py if running from source)
  3. The application will automatically open the browser at http://localhost:8000

Step 3: Initial Configuration

  1. On the Home page, click ⚙️ Settings (or navigate directly to the Settings page in the sidebar)
  2. In the OpenRGB section, verify that Host is 127.0.0.1 and Port is 6742
  3. Click "Test Connection" — you should see ✓ Connected with the number of detected devices
  4. In the Elite Dangerous section, set the path to the Journal folder (e.g. C:\Users\[Username]\Saved Games\Frontier Developments\Elite Dangerous)
  5. Click "Save Settings"

Step 4: First Test Effect

  1. Navigate to the Devices page in the sidebar
  2. You should see the list of your RGB devices detected by OpenRGB
  3. Click "Test" on a device — you will see a 2-second rainbow effect
  4. If it works, the setup is complete! 🎉

1.4 First Setup: Recommended Workflow

To configure ED:Event2Led from scratch after installation, follow this recommended workflow:

  1. Settings — Verify OpenRGB connection and journal path
  2. Devices — Enable/disable devices and test RGB
  3. Device Topology — Draw where the LED strips are (required for the Ambilight)
  4. Ambilight (optional) — Enable the devices around the monitor
  5. Effects Library — Explore built-in effects or create new ones
  6. Event Mapping — Assign effects to Elite Dangerous events
  7. Home Dashboard — Start journal monitoring and play!

3. Effects Library

auto_awesome

Multi-Step Sequencer

The Effects Editor lets you create complex RGB animations by combining 10+ steps. Each step uses one of the 17 available effect types with customizable parameters.

3.1 What is a Visual Effect

In ED:Event2Led, a Visual Effect is a complete RGB animation sequence composed of multiple steps.

Example: The "Hyperspace Jump" effect could have this sequence:

  1. Step 1: Blue Wave (2 seconds) — Simulates FSD charging
  2. Step 2: White Explosion (1 second) — Jump moment
  3. Step 3: Dim blue Static color (3 seconds) — Hyperspace tunnel
  4. Step 4: Green Bloom (1.5 seconds) — Arrival in the new system

3.1.1 Important Terminology

Term Description
Visual Effect Complete sequence saved in the library (e.g. "Hyperspace Jump")
Step Single step of the sequence (numbered card 1, 2, 3...)
Effect Type Rendering algorithm (Wave, Bloom, Fire, etc.) — 17 available
Parameters Step configuration (colors, duration, direction, speed)

3.5 Advanced Parameters

3.5.1 Use Normalized Coordinates

Available for 15 spatial effects. When enabled:

  • Device-independent effects — Same effect on keyboard, mouse, strip with correct proportions
  • Multi-device sync — Universal center point (0.5, 0.5) = always geometric "center"
  • Parameter consistency — Direction "right" behaves the same on all devices

When to use it: If you want the effect to appear identical on devices of different shapes.

3.5.2 Direction (8-Way)

Many effects support 8 directions:

  • Cardinal: top, down, left, right
  • Diagonal: tl (top-left), tr (top-right), bl (bottom-left), br (bottom-right)

3×3 grid in the Inspector for visual selection.

3.5.3 Loop and Loop Count

  • Loop Infinite — Repeats the step indefinitely until manually cancelled
  • Loop Count — Repeats the step N times (e.g. loop_count=3 for Explosion)
warning

Warning: Loop Infinite blocks the sequence

If you enable "Loop Infinite" on a step, subsequent steps will NEVER be executed! Use Loop Infinite only for continuous ambient effects (e.g. Plasma, Aurora) or as the last step in the sequence.

3.6 Post-Behavior

After the sequence ends, you can configure the final behavior:

Behavior Description
Static Color Holds the last frame of the animation (default)
Turn Off Turns off all LEDs (fade to black)
Loop Effect Repeats the entire sequence from the beginning

4. Event Mapping

bolt

Event Triggers

Event Mapping links Elite Dangerous events (FSDJump, Docked, UnderAttack, etc.) to RGB effects in your library. When an event occurs in-game, the assigned effect activates automatically.

4.1 What is Event Mapping

Elite Dangerous writes in-game events to JSON files called Journal. Every action (jump, docking, combat, scan) generates an event with a timestamp and data.

ED:Event2Led monitors the journal in real time and, when it detects a mapped event, activates the corresponding RGB effect.

4.1.1 Journal Event Example

When you jump to hyperspace, Elite generates this event:

{
  "timestamp": "2026-03-26T20:35:12Z",
  "event": "FSDJump",
  "StarSystem": "Deciat",
  "StarPos": [123.4, -56.7, 89.0],
  "SystemAddress": 8605315456789
}

If you have mapped FSDJump → "Hyperspace Jump Effect", the effect will activate automatically.

4.2 Mapping Interface

The Events page shows a table with all available events (100+ events):

Column Description
Event Technical name of the event (e.g. "FSDJump")
Assigned Effect Name of the mapped RGB effect (or "—" if not mapped)
Mini Timeline Visual preview of the step sequence (colors + proportional duration)
Priority Level 1-10 (10 = highest, cancels effects with lower priority)
Enabled ON/OFF toggle to enable/disable the mapping
Actions Edit, Clear, Test buttons

4.3 Assigning an Effect to an Event

Step 1: Find the Event

  1. Use the Search bar at the top to filter events (e.g. search "jump")
  2. Scroll the table to find the desired event

Step 2: Click "Assign Effect"

  1. Click the "Assign Effect" button on the event row
  2. The Effect Selection Modal opens

Step 3: Select Effect

  1. In the modal, you see a grid with all effects from the library
  2. Each card shows: name, mini timeline, total duration
  3. Click on an effect to select it
  4. Use the Search bar in the modal to filter effects

    • 4-6: Medium events (Scan, CargoScoop, LandingGearDeployed)
    • 1-3: Ambient events (FSSSignalDiscovered, Music)

Step 5: Save

  1. Click "Assign" in the modal
  2. The mapping is saved and the table updates
  3. The event now shows the assigned effect and the mini timeline

4.4 Priority System

When two events occur simultaneously (or one interrupts the other), the priority system decides which effect wins.

4.4.1 Priority Rules

  1. Higher priority wins — If an event arrives with priority ≥ current effect, the new effect CANCELS the current one and starts
  2. Lower priority is ignored — If an event arrives with priority < current effect, it is IGNORED
  3. Auto-reset after duration — After an effect ends, the system resets the priority and accepts new events

4.4.2 Practical Example

Scenario:

  1. A Scan effect plays (priority 5, duration 3 seconds)
  2. After 1 second, UnderAttack arrives (priority 9)
  3. Result: Scan is CANCELLED immediately, UnderAttack starts

Scenario 2:

  1. An UnderAttack effect plays (priority 9, duration 5 seconds)
  2. After 2 seconds, Scan arrives (priority 5)
  3. Result: Scan is IGNORED, UnderAttack continues
tips_and_updates

  • 10: Emergencies (HullCritical, CanopyBreach)
  • 9: Combat (UnderAttack, ShieldsDown, EnemySpotted)

12. Ambilight

info

The Ambilight colors the strips around your monitor with the colors on the screen.

It works in the background and pauses on its own when an effect starts: as soon as the effect ends, it picks up again.

12.1 Before You Start

  1. Draw your strips in the Topology Editor (chapter 6): the Ambilight only uses devices that have a topology.
  2. In the same editor, place the screen rectangle around the monitor strips (6.7). It decides which side of the screen each LED reads: the zone name doesn't matter.
  3. Come back here, enable the devices and turn on the service.
tips_and_updates

Keyboards are excluded from the Ambilight: they have their own section (Keyboard Designer).

12.2 Status and Activation

  • Enable: turns the service on or off. The indicator shows ACTIVE or INACTIVE.
  • Test idle: stops any running effects and hands the LEDs straight back to the Ambilight, so you can try it without waiting for effects to end. If the LEDs don't change, a notification explains why (service off, no device enabled).
  • The Ambilight also pauses during calibration and during the identify flash in the Topology Editor.

12.3 Screen Capture

Option When to use it
Auto (WGC → DXGI → mss) Recommended: tries the methods in order and uses the first one that works.
WGC Windows Graphics Capture: also works with browsers and accelerated windows.
DXGI For games in exclusive fullscreen.
mss CPU only: the most compatible, and the slowest.

Below the menu, Active: shows the method actually in use.

12.4 FPS

How many times per second the LEDs are updated (1-60). Next to it you see the real FPS. Higher values are more responsive but put more load on the CPU and OpenRGB: 15-30 is a good balance.

12.5 Sampling Strategy

How the color of each LED is chosen from the screen pixels:

Strategy Description
Adaptive + EMA (best) Adaptive depth and temporal smoothing together.
Adaptive depth The depth of the area read adapts to the content: if the edge of the screen is uniform (black bars, flat interface) it reads deeper, if it is full of detail it stays close to the edge.
Percentile + EMA Percentile 80% with temporal smoothing.
Percentile 80% Mean of the brightest 20% of the pixels in the area: vivid colors, ignores dark areas, and small changing details (a blinking cursor) move the color slightly instead of making it jump.
Dominant color The median color of the area: stable, little affected by small details.
RGB mean The simple average of the pixels: the most faithful, but it tends toward gray.

12.6 Sampling Area

  • With fixed-depth strategies, Depth is how far in from the edge the screen is read, as a % of the screen's shorter side.
  • With adaptive strategies, Depth min and max appear instead: the limits within which the depth adapts.

12.7 Temporal Smoothing

  • EMA Alpha: how quickly the LEDs follow the screen. 0.05 = very smooth, 0.95 = instant. If the colors flicker, lower it.
  • Scene-cut threshold: if the color changes by more than this threshold (for example a camera cut), the LED jumps straight to the new color instead of fading.

These settings apply to the strategies with EMA.

12.8 Output

Saturation boost makes colors more vivid: 1.0 = original colors, 2.5 = maximum. LEDs tend to look dull compared to the monitor, so a value between 1.2 and 1.6 usually looks better.

12.9 Devices

  • Each device has an Ambilight switch. It is disabled if the device has no topology: in that case you are invited to set one up in the Topology Editor.
  • When a device is enabled, its zones appear below it, each with its own switch, so you can leave some out.
  • A zone entirely outside the screen rectangle is marked Outside the screen and can't be enabled. If none of a device's zones is on the screen, a warning appears.
Where the LED is What the Ambilight does
Near a side of the screen rectangle Takes the color of the screen edge on that side, in the stretch facing the LED.
Inside the rectangle Takes the color of the point of the screen under it.
Outside the rectangle Leaves it alone.

12.10 Troubleshooting

The LEDs don't light up

  • Is the service enabled, and does at least one device have its Ambilight switch on?
  • Is an effect running? The Ambilight restarts when it ends: use Test idle to try it right away.
  • Are the zones on the screen rectangle? The ones outside are not used.

The colors are on the wrong side

  • The side is decided by the position of the LEDs relative to the screen rectangle in the Topology Editor (6.7). Turn on calibration to check that the drawing matches your desk.

The LEDs stay black with a fullscreen game

  • Choose DXGI capture.

The colors flicker

  • Use a strategy with EMA and lower EMA Alpha, or reduce the FPS.

Appendix D: FAQ

Installation & Setup

Q: Does ED:Event2Led work without Elite Dangerous installed?

A: Yes, but only for configuration and hardware testing. Journal monitoring requires Elite to be installed and running.

Q: Can I use ED:Event2Led with other games?

A: No, it is specific to Elite Dangerous. The application reads Elite's proprietary journal format.

Q: Is OpenRGB required?

A: Yes, ED:Event2Led uses the OpenRGB SDK to control RGB hardware. There is no alternative at this time.

Hardware Compatibility

Q: Is my RGB keyboard supported?

A: If OpenRGB detects your keyboard, ED:Event2Led can control it. Check compatibility at openrgb.org.

Q: Can I use ED:Event2Led alongside Razer Synapse / Corsair iCUE?

A: No, vendor-specific software (Synapse, iCUE, Aura) blocks OpenRGB. Close Synapse/iCUE before starting OpenRGB.

Q: How many RGB devices can I control simultaneously?

A: There is no hard limit, but USB bandwidth constrains performance. Setups with 500+ total LEDs may experience lag. 4-6 devices is optimal.

Performance & Troubleshooting

Q: Effects have visible flickering/lag

A: Common causes:

  • Too many active devices → Disable non-essential devices
  • Insufficient USB bandwidth → Use a powered USB 3.0 hub
  • CPU overload → Close heavy background applications

Q: OpenRGB disconnects randomly during gameplay

A: Elite Dangerous in exclusive fullscreen mode can cause conflicts. Solution:

  1. Elite → Graphics Options → Display Mode → Borderless Window
  2. OpenRGB → Settings → "Start minimized to tray"

Q: Effects do not trigger on specific events

A: Check:

  1. Activity Log → Verify that the event arrives from the journal
  2. Event Mapping → Verify the mapping is configured and Enabled=ON
  3. Priority → Raise priority to 8-9 if other effects are blocking it

Effects & Mapping

Q: Can I assign multiple effects to the same event?

A: No, each event can have only 1 mapped effect. Use multi-step sequences for complex animations.

Q: How do I create effects that react to multiple flags simultaneously (e.g. ShieldsDown + LowFuel)?

A: Not natively supported. Workaround: use different priorities to create a "cascade" of effects.

Q: Can I create custom effects with Python code?

A: Technically yes (open-source code), but it requires knowledge of Python and the project architecture. There is no UI for custom code.

Keyboard & Presets

Q: Do keyboard presets persist after a PC reboot?

A: Yes, they are saved in config/keyboard_presets.json. Automatic backup on every save.

Q: Can I export keyboard presets and share them with friends?

A: Yes, use "Export Configuration" (Settings). It includes keyboard presets plus the entire configuration.

Q: Does Keyboard Designer work with non-QWERTY keyboards (AZERTY, QWERTZ)?

A: Yes, it displays the standard US ANSI layout but controls LEDs independently of the physical keyboard layout.

Topology & Layout

Q: Do I need to configure topology for every LED strip?

A: A straight strip also works without one, but the topology is required for the Ambilight and lets spatial effects follow where your strips really are. It takes a few minutes in the Topology Editor (chapter 6).

Q: Can I use topology for keyboards?

A: Keyboards automatically use the native 2D matrix. Custom topology for keyboards is not supported (and not necessary).

Multilanguage

Q: Can I contribute translations for other languages?

A: Yes! Translation files are in web/config/i18n/. Pull requests are welcome on GitHub.

Q: Do translations include effect and event names as well?

A: Yes, all 5 languages have complete translations for the UI, default effects, event categories, and the manual.

    • Import requires a full restart to apply changes

Language does not change after saving

Symptoms:

  • Save Settings → Language remains the same
  • Only some labels change language, others do not

Solutions:

  1. Hard refresh browser:
    • Ctrl + F5 to force a full reload
  2. Browser cache:
    • Clear browser cache (Ctrl + Shift + Delete)
    • Restart browser and reload localhost:8000
  3. Verify translation file:
    • Check that web/config/i18n/[lang].json exists
    • If missing, reinstall ED:Event2Led
